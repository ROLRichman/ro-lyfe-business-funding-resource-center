Philadelphia Small Business Catalyst Fund

Competitive grants up to $50,000 plus advisory support for qualifying Philadelphia businesses with a measurable growth plan. Basic criteria include physical Philadelphia business location, City tax compliance, at least $10,000 annual revenue, two years of tax returns, and revenue under $2 million.

Not for real estate acquisition. Certain lending and passive rental businesses are excluded. Broker activity should be reviewed with the program before applying.

Recommended first move for Root Of Lyfe: Request a no-cost Temple SBDC consultation. Explain the LLC's actual business activities and revenue sources, then ask the consultant to assess which grant and business-development programs fit. Specifically ask whether the company's funding-broker/referral activities create any Catalyst Fund eligibility issue before investing time in that application.
Important: Grants are competitive and never guaranteed. Root Of Lyfe is a business resource and referral operation—not a government agency, grant-maker, lender, law firm, or tax advisor. Funding decisions belong to the program or lender. Never charge a percentage of a grant award or imply that payment can secure approval. Any paid writing, consulting, or referral arrangement should have a written scope, transparent fee, conflict disclosure, and appropriate professional/legal review.
Frequently asked questions

Know the difference

Does a grant counselor give me grant money?

No. A counselor or SBDC advisor helps assess readiness, identify programs, and prepare. The grant administrator decides eligibility and awards.

Can I pay a grant writer only after I qualify?

You can request a no-cost initial screen and written scope before paying for application work. Some firms may offer milestone billing, but it must be agreed in writing. Do not pay a percentage of an award or a fee for guaranteed approval.

Can Root Of Lyfe help other startups apply?

Potentially, within a clearly defined service scope: opportunity research, readiness checklists, document organization, application drafting support, and referrals to qualified advisors. Confirm program rules, privacy practices, professional boundaries, and fee disclosures before offering paid services.

Should every business apply for grants?

No. Some businesses are better served by loans, CDFIs, technical assistance, procurement opportunities, accelerators, or customer revenue. Match the funding type to the business need.
